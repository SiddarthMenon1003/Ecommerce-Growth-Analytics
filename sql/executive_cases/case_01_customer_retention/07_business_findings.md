# Executive Findings

## Case Overview

This analysis investigated customer acquisition, retention, purchasing behaviour, and lifetime value using the Brazilian Olist e-commerce marketplace dataset. The objective was to understand whether sustainable revenue growth is primarily driven by acquiring new customers or by retaining existing ones, and to identify the customer behaviours that have the greatest impact on long-term business performance.

The analysis was conducted using a production-style analytics pipeline consisting of an ETL process, a PostgreSQL data warehouse, dimensional modelling, and six analytical SQL studies covering data validation, exploratory analysis, cohort analysis, retention, repeat purchasing, and customer lifetime value.

---

# Executive Summary

The marketplace demonstrates strong customer acquisition capabilities but relatively weak customer retention. Although the platform has successfully attracted a large customer base, repeat purchasing behaviour remains limited, resulting in a business model that depends heavily on continuously acquiring new customers to sustain revenue growth.

Customer cohort analysis indicates that engagement declines rapidly after the initial purchase, while repeat purchase analysis shows that the overwhelming majority of customers transact only once. Lifetime value analysis further reveals that a very small proportion of customers contributes disproportionately to long-term revenue generation.

Collectively, these findings suggest that improving customer retention presents a larger long-term growth opportunity than increasing acquisition spend alone.

---

# Key Finding 1 – Marketplace Growth

The marketplace has successfully scaled its customer base and transaction volume, processing nearly one hundred thousand customer orders across multiple Brazilian regions. Geographic analysis indicates broad customer distribution, demonstrating that the platform has achieved significant national reach rather than relying on isolated regional markets.

From a business perspective, customer acquisition does not currently appear to be the primary operational constraint. The platform has already demonstrated its ability to attract new customers at scale.

---

# Key Finding 2 – Customer Retention

Monthly cohort analysis reveals that customer retention declines substantially following the initial purchase period. While every acquisition cohort begins with full participation during the month of first purchase, relatively few customers continue purchasing in subsequent months.

This behaviour suggests that customer relationships are generally transactional rather than recurring. Without targeted retention initiatives, the business must continually replace departing customers with newly acquired ones in order to maintain revenue levels.

---

# Key Finding 3 – Repeat Purchase Behaviour

Analysis of customer purchasing frequency indicates that approximately 97% of customers place only a single order throughout their observed lifetime, while only a small minority become repeat purchasers.

This represents one of the strongest findings in the entire study.

The marketplace currently functions as a high-acquisition, low-loyalty business. Although acquisition efforts appear effective, customer loyalty remains comparatively weak, limiting the long-term value generated from marketing investments.

---

# Key Finding 4 – Customer Lifetime Value

Customer lifetime value is highly concentrated within a relatively small group of repeat customers.

While most customers contribute only a single transaction, a small segment continues purchasing multiple times and generates substantially greater cumulative revenue over time. These customers represent the marketplace's highest-value assets and are likely responsible for a disproportionate share of overall profitability.

This concentration highlights the importance of identifying, retaining, and expanding relationships with high-value customer segments.

---

# Key Finding 5 – Customer Value Distribution

Lifetime value analysis demonstrates considerable variation across the customer base. The majority of customers remain within lower-value segments, while only a limited number progress into higher-value customer tiers.

This imbalance suggests that the marketplace currently excels at acquiring customers but has fewer mechanisms for increasing customer value after the initial transaction.

Improving average customer lifetime value would likely generate stronger long-term financial performance than focusing exclusively on customer acquisition volume.

---

# Overall Business Assessment

From an operational perspective, the marketplace appears healthy and capable of attracting new customers at scale. However, sustainable long-term growth will depend less on acquisition and more on improving customer retention, increasing purchase frequency, and expanding customer lifetime value.

The analytical evidence consistently indicates that retention represents the largest opportunity for future business growth. Investments aimed at encouraging second purchases, strengthening customer loyalty, and developing high-value customer relationships are therefore expected to deliver significantly greater long-term returns than acquisition-focused strategies alone.

---

# Closing Statement

This analysis establishes a comprehensive understanding of customer behaviour across the entire customer lifecycle—from acquisition through retention and lifetime value. The findings provide a quantitative foundation for evaluating future retention initiatives and support the development of targeted growth strategies designed to maximize long-term customer value.
